What is a SIP?

SIP stands for Systematic Investment Plan. It means investing a fixed amount every month in a mutual fund — regardless of market conditions. Over time, this builds significant wealth through the power of rupee cost averaging and compounding.

For example: ₹5,000/month for 20 years at 12% annual return gives a corpus of over ₹49 lakh — from a total investment of only ₹12 lakh. The remaining ₹37 lakh is pure returns from compounding.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good SIP amount to start with?

You can start with as little as ₹500/month. However, ₹5,000/month in an index fund like Nifty 50 for 15–20 years is considered a solid long-term plan for wealth creation.

What return rate should I use for SIP calculation?

For Nifty 50 index funds, a conservative estimate of 10–12% p.a. is commonly used. For actively managed large-cap funds, 12–15% p.a. For mid-cap/small-cap, 15–18% p.a. (with higher risk).

Is SIP better than lump sum investment?

SIP is generally better for regular salaried investors because it removes the risk of timing the market. Lump sum can outperform if invested at a market bottom, but SIP gives consistent results over long periods.
