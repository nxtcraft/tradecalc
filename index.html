How traders use these calculators

Risk management decides whether a trader survives long enough to be profitable, and these calculators turn the main rules into numbers. The Position Size Calculator uses the formula: quantity = (capital × risk percentage) ÷ (entry price − stop-loss price). For example, with Rs 5,00,000 capital, 1% risk, an entry at Rs 500 and a stop-loss at Rs 490, the risk per trade is Rs 5,000 and the position size is 500 shares.

The Risk/Reward Calculator compares potential profit with potential loss. The Brokerage Calculator adds up charges such as brokerage, STT, SEBI charges and stamp duty to show your true cost per trade, and the P&L and lot size calculators help you plan exits and sizing. Costs matter because small, frequent trades can lose a large share of profit to charges.

How to use it

  1. Pick the calculator that matches your question.
  2. Enter your capital, risk percentage, entry price and stop-loss, or the details the tool asks for.
  3. Read the result and test different stop-loss levels to see how position size and risk change.
  4. Record each trade in a journal so you can review your results.

Good to know

  • Many traders risk only 1 to 2% of capital on a single trade.
  • Look for a risk-to-reward ratio of at least 1:2 so that winners can pay for losers.
  • Check charges before choosing a strategy that trades very often.
  • Derivatives such as futures and options can lose more than you invest. SEBI studies have found that the large majority of individual F&O traders lose money.
