What is Position Sizing?

Position sizing answers the most important question in trading: how much to buy. It is the process of deciding how many shares, lots, or units to purchase based on your account size and how much you are willing to lose if the trade goes against you.

Professional traders never risk more than 1–2% of their account on a single trade. If your account is ₹5,00,000 and you risk 1%, your maximum loss on any trade is ₹5,000. The position size calculator does the math for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 1% rule in trading?

The 1% rule means you should never risk more than 1% of your trading account on a single trade. If your account is ₹10 lakh, maximum risk per trade is ₹10,000. This protects you from large drawdowns even after a string of losing trades.

How is position size calculated?

Position Size = Risk Amount ÷ Risk per Share. Where Risk Amount = Account Size × Risk %, and Risk per Share = Entry Price − Stop Loss. For example: ₹5L account, 1% risk, entry ₹500, SL ₹480 → Risk = ₹5,000, Risk/share = ₹20, Shares = 250.

Should I always use a stop loss?

Yes. A stop loss defines your maximum risk and makes position sizing possible. Without a stop loss, you cannot calculate position size, and you cannot control your risk.
